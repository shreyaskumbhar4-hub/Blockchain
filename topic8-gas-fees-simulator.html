How the network allocates resources

A transaction consumes computational work and competes for a place in a block. Try changing congestion and your tip above to see how the queue responds.

01 · Computational Weight

Different operations consume different amounts of gas. A simple ETH transfer commonly uses 21,000 gas units, while complex smart-contract execution can consume much more.

02 · The Mempool

The mempool is a temporary waiting area for pending transactions. Validators or block builders choose eligible transactions while respecting block gas limits and other constraints.

03 · Supply & Demand

Block space is limited. When demand rises, users may offer a higher priority fee to improve the chance of earlier inclusion. A higher tip does not guarantee inclusion.

Follow the path from transaction submission to block inclusion.

STEP 1 · BROADCASTUsers sign and broadcast transactions with a gas limit, max fee, and priority tip.
STEP 2 · MEMPOOLPending transactions wait while nodes check validity and maintain their transaction pools.
STEP 3 · SELECTIONBlock builders select transactions, considering effective tips, gas usage, and block capacity.
STEP 4 · CONFIRMATIONSelected transactions are included in a block and executed by the network.

Understanding the fee calculation

For an EIP-1559 transaction, the effective gas price is based on the base fee and the effective priority fee, capped by the sender's max fee.

Transaction fee = Gas used × Effective gas price
Effective gas price = Base fee + Effective priority fee

Base Fee

A protocol-determined fee per gas unit that adjusts according to recent block demand. The base fee is burned on Ethereum mainnet.

Priority Fee (Tip)

An optional incentive paid to the block proposer or builder path to encourage transaction inclusion. Higher tips can improve priority during busy periods.

Gas Limit

The maximum amount of computation a transaction is allowed to use. Actual gas used determines the execution fee; unused gas is not charged as used computation.
